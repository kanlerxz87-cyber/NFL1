Official depth charts, parsed faithfully, and a ledger that can be replayed

Six official club depth-chart pages were read on 15 November 2026 and stored byte for byte. Each is parsed so that a slot is a group of the players the club printed in it, a jersey number is separated from the name and kept, and an empty cell stays empty. Every capture is entered in an append-only ledger with its fetch clock time and the sha256 of its bytes, so a depth chart or an injury designation can be replayed as it stood when it was read. Nothing on this page is inferred, and where an official source does not publish a value the cell says so.

1. What each official capture produced

One row per official page read this session. "Capture kind" distinguishes a page that prints table rows from one that prints only an image. A jersey count is the number of cells where a leading number was removed from the name and stored in its own field. A shared slot is a cell where the club printed two players at the same rank; it is kept whole. A gap is a fact the official page does not publish in a readable form; it is recorded and never filled in.

5. The immutable availability ledger

Each capture is its own directory, named with the first 12 characters of the sha256 of its own bytes, holding raw.txt and record.json. A capture is never rewritten: re-reading the same page writes a second, separately hashed capture. That is what makes a designation replayable as it was known. python3 pipeline/ledger.py verify re-hashes all 12 and re-derives every stored count.

6. Depth rows the official transactions log contradicts

A depth chart is a club opinion with no expiry date; a transactions log is a dated legal fact. A row is flagged stale when the chart still names a player whom the log placed on reserve and no later dated line returns him. Nothing is corrected on either side: both sources are published as they stand. A transactions line often reports several unrelated moves, so the comparison is made clause by clause — a player only inherits the move in the clause that names him.
