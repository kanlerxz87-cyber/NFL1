What this says

Two baseline strategies reach 60.7% straight-up accuracy, which looks impressive in isolation (p = 0.0156). But four strategies were tested, so the honest threshold is the Holm-adjusted p-value of 0.0626 — not significant. Calibration is also poor in the extreme bucket (predicted 19%, observed 39%), and Brier skill is -0.038, i.e. worse than always saying 50/50.

Picking winners is materially easier than covering a spread. A ~60% straight-up hit rate is fully consistent with an average-accuracy forecaster and says nothing about beating a price.

No official NFL odds source is reachable from this environment and none is stored in the repo. P/L figures are a sensitivity scenario, not realised returns, and cannot be used to claim profitability.

Correlation findings that change decisions

  • Home advantage is absent in this sample. Home teams won 49.6% of decided games (95% CI 41.2%–58.1%), which covers 50%. Any strategy that implicitly assumes a home edge is leaning on a factor the verified data does not currently support.
  • Points scored tracks opponent quality (r = +0.386). This is real dependence and is why legs from the same game must never be multiplied as if independent.
  • Margin and total are not independent enough to assume away (r = -0.161, p = 0.061) — treat spread + total in one ticket as a dependent pair until a larger sample says otherwise.
