A correlation_ratio above 1.00 means the two legs landed together more often than independence predicts. Because a standard parlay is priced at the product of the legs, positively correlated legs make the parlay cheaper than it should be -- which is why some pairs show a positive return even though neither leg does on its own. That is a property of the pricing rule, not proof of skill, and it is why every pair is published rather than the best one. The product of two legs is what a book would pay on a standard parlay. Because the legs are correlated, that price is only fair if the correlation is zero; the correlation_ratio column shows how far from zero it is. 66 pairs tested, 1 survive correction for multiple testing at the 5% level. The dependence ratio is the observed joint hit rate divided by the product of the marginal hit rates: 1.0000 means the legs behave as if they were independent.

These are the correlations that decide whether a same-game parlay is fairly priced. A book that prices a two-leg same-game parlay as if the legs were independent is underpricing it whenever r is strongly positive, and the table shows where that is true.

Why no parlay on this site is a recommendation

  • Every price used is taken from the source file for each leg. No -110 assumption is made anywhere.
  • Legs are combined only when both fired in the same week in different games, so the independence question is about week effects, not about one play causing another.
  • P-values are corrected across all 66 pairs with Benjamini-Hochberg. Testing dozens of parlays and reporting the best one manufactures a winner; the correction charges for that.
  • UNVERIFIED / BLANK: no official or book-level price exists for any player prop in this repository's sources, so no prop bet is scored and no profit is claimed. Projections and correlations only.
