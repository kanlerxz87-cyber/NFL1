Signals & evidence

Does a personnel loss tell you anything the price has not already told you? This page answers that with a regression, not an opinion: the result of a game is regressed on the closing spread alone, then on the closing spread plus the measure. What the measure adds is the whole test.

Sources: nflverse schedules and stats releases and the official injury report. Inputs are committed, pinned by provenance, and re-runnable with s9cb/analysis/run_personnel.py.

Games analysed 7,104 with both a closing line and a result
Information tests 13 corrected together
Significant after correction 0 Benjamini-Hochberg, 5%
Seasons covered 2019-2026 player-week data; game results from 1999

The result, stated without decoration. None of the 13 measures tested adds statistically significant information beyond the closing spread once the correction for 13 tests is applied.

The smallest raw p belongs to away ruled-out offensive players: raw p=0.0106, corrected p=0.138. It is reported here precisely because it is the best-looking number in the table, and it still does not clear the bar.

The closing-spread coefficient sits between 1.03 to 1.05 with p effectively zero in every row, which is the check that the join and the specification are right. Without that, a null result could just be a broken regression.

Known limits of this analysis

  • The committed extract has no defensive player statistics, so a defensive deficiency is measured in ruled-out players, not in lost production.
  • The official report lists game status; it does not list who replaced whom. Replacement identity comes from the depth chart on the main site, not here.
  • Practice-squad promotions and in-game injuries are not in the report.
  • The 2026 season is in progress: its rows are small-sample and are shown as such.
