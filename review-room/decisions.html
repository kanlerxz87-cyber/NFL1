D01 · Which site generation is the one published product?

blocked Architecture waiting on: Q28, G01

Six generations of this site now exist in one repository (root dashboard, pages/, lab/, research/, verified-lab/, referee/, dashboard/). The same question is answered differently on different generations, so a reader cannot tell which number is current. Every later decision depends on a single answer being current.

Option A — Declare the root dashboard authoritative and redirect the rest recommended

Buys
One number per question, reachable from one place. Readers stop comparing generations.
Costs
Low: one nav entry per generation plus a banner on each superseded page.
Could go wrong
Working pages from other sessions become second-class; their extra detail is harder to find.
Effort
1 session

Option B — Declare the research section authoritative and redirect the rest

Buys
The biggest evidence base becomes the front door.
Costs
Medium: the research section carries the largest samples and the gate matrix, but not the official-source depth-chart join.
Could go wrong
The official-source verification pages (the ones that made every value auditable) fall behind a redirect.
Effort
1 session

Option C — Keep all generations and publish a comparison table on the landing page

Buys
Nothing is deleted; every session's work stays visible.
Costs
Ongoing: every change must be made in the place(s) that own it.
Could go wrong
Duplicated, drifting numbers remain the reader's problem, which is the current state.
Effort
Ongoing tax
Why option A: The root dashboard is the only generation whose CI regenerates it from the data layer and whose figures an independent audit recomputes (47 checks). Its weakness is breadth, not correctness. Option C preserves the current defect.

What you have to do: Say which letter you want; nothing is redirected until you do.

D02 · Buy a licensed odds feed, or publish without one?

blocked Data waiting on: Q01 (price, book, timestamp), G02

The project wants to originate its own lines and rank strategies for reward. Without a timestamped, book-level price there is no closing-line value, no prop pricing and no honest ROI. Everything currently published is scored against a mirrored closing price, not against a price a bettor could have taken.

Option A — Licence one odds API and store every quote append-only recommended

Buys
Real prices at real times: CLV, prop markets, parlay pricing and honest ROI all become possible.
Costs
Recurring fee, contract, integration; probably the largest single cost in this project.
Could go wrong
Vendor terms can restrict redistribution, which would affect publishing every price on a public site.
Effort
2-3 sessions plus a contract

Option B — Publish model lines only, no ROI or leaderboard rewards

Buys
Every published claim stays supportable.
Costs
Nothing to buy; drop the reward and ROI claims from the site.
Could go wrong
The leaderboard, which is the point of the project, becomes an accuracy contest rather than a profit contest.
Effort
1 session

Option C — Keep the mirrored closing line and label every result as indicative

Buys
Large samples now (7,103 played games, 5,228 priced).
Costs
Already paid.
Could go wrong
It answers 'did the model beat the close', not 'could you have made money'. Those are different questions and the site must not blur them.
Effort
Done, if labelled
Why option A: The project's stated goal (originate lines, reward top strategies) cannot be measured honestly without prices. Until A is funded, C is the only defensible publication mode and the label has to be on every table.

What you have to do: Approve or decline the licence; if declined, choose B or C.

D03 · What exactly does the leaderboard reward, and can it be paid?

blocked Competition waiting on: Q20 (what rewards), Q27, G03 legal review

A reward on a leaderboard is a policy statement, and if it is money it is a regulated activity in most US states. No official source reviewed defines a reward formula, and the site currently publishes none.

Option A — No money: rank and badge only

Buys
No licensing, no jurisdiction problem, no payout accounting.
Costs
Low. A ranking rule and a badge.
Could go wrong
Weaker incentive; the leaderboard becomes editorial rather than a contest.
Effort
1 session

Option B — Prize pool with an entry rule and full terms

Buys
A real contest, which is what the brief asks for.
Costs
High: legal review of every state the site can be read in, payout accounting, tax reporting, dispute process.
Could go wrong
Likely requires a licence or exemption in several jurisdictions; the project may not be eligible at all.
Effort
Multi-month, outside a code session

Option C — Rank-only now, with the reward formula pre-registered for later recommended

Buys
The ranking rule can be frozen now, so scores cannot be tuned to whoever is winning.
Costs
Low.
Could go wrong
Formula announced but unpayable until a decision is made.
Effort
1 session
Why option C: Pre-registering the formula is the part that protects the science, and it costs nothing. Paying is a legal question this analysis cannot answer, so it stays open rather than being guessed.

What you have to do: Choose A or B; C happens automatically if nothing is chosen.

D04 · How should the personnel-deficiency signal be used, now that it has been tested against the price?

ready Signals waiting on: nothing

This session tested thirteen personnel measures against the closing line on up to 7,083 played games, and separately asked whether the market's own number already contains them. The measures are readable and descriptive — who is out, who is starting in place of whom — but as a group they did not show a reliable information edge after correction, and none of the five bet rules built on them passed the gates. The live counts and corrected p-values are on the Signals and Week 10 pages; they are read from the data files, not repeated here. Deciding what that negative result means for the project is the owner's call, not the analysis's.

Option A — Keep the deficiency register as a descriptive dashboard and publish no personnel-driven bets recommended

Buys
Honest presentation: the register answers 'who is out and who is next', not 'who covers'.
Costs
None: already built.
Could go wrong
The brief asked for personnel signals; this answers it with a negative result, which is still an answer.
Effort
Done

Option B — Collect player-level defensive data and retest with a production-loss measure on defence

Buys
A real test of the '2nd/3rd/4th string defender' question instead of a count of names on a report.
Costs
One collection change plus one analysis pass; needs per-player defensive snaps and statistics, which the current extract lacks.
Could go wrong
Likely also a negative result; the market prices injuries quickly and publicly.
Effort
1 session

Option C — Test the reaction instead of the level: how fast does the line move after a designation?

Buys
The realistic question: the information is public, so the edge can only be in speed.
Costs
Needs a timestamped odds history, which is D02.
Could go wrong
Blocked until D02 is decided; unmeasurable with closing lines only.
Effort
Blocked
Why option A: A is what the evidence supports today and costs nothing. B is worth doing only as a deliberate second attempt, and C is the only option with a plausible route to an edge, but it depends on D02.

What you have to do: Accept the negative result, or commission B or C.

D05 · Stop publishing implied or model-only numbers where a verified value is missing?

ready Integrity waiting on: nothing

The brief says: if a value cannot be confirmed from an official source, leave it blank and flag it. The site mostly obeys this, but prop market columns are model-only and several legacy pages publish derived figures with no official counterpart. Deciding the rule once fixes every page instead of page by page.

Option A — Hard rule: a cell is either official-sourced or blank with a flag

Buys
One rule, mechanically checkable, no exceptions to argue about.
Costs
Some tables shrink visibly; some pages lose their point.
Could go wrong
Model output (own lines, projections) is not official by definition, so it must be labelled as model output rather than deleted.
Effort
1 session

Option B — Two classes: official facts, and model estimates clearly marked recommended

Buys
Keeps the model content while never presenting it as an official value.
Costs
Costs a per-cell or per-column marker.
Could go wrong
Reader must respect the distinction; marking has to be consistent or it degrades.
Effort
1-2 sessions
Why option B: The site's purpose includes originating lines, which are by definition not official. B keeps that purpose while obeying the blank-and-flag rule for anything claiming to be a fact.

What you have to do: Pick A or B; B is the default if the model pages are to survive.

D06 · What is the publication gate for a number that changed after it was verified?

ready Operations waiting on: nothing

This session found the committed standings were one game stale (the Week 10 Thursday game was missing) and that the official injury page changes during the day. A published value can therefore be correct when verified and wrong when read. The repository has no rule for that.

Option A — Show an as-of timestamp and the source's own refresh window on every time-sensitive table recommended

Buys
The reader can tell how old a number is and whether it can still change.
Costs
Low: the timestamps already exist in the data files.
Could go wrong
None material; a timestamp does not fix staleness, it only discloses it.
Effort
1 session

Option B — Refresh time-sensitive sources immediately before each publication and block the build if older than a threshold

Buys
A stale number cannot be published silently.
Costs
Medium: a refresh step and a freshness check in CI.
Could go wrong
A source that is offline blocks publication of everything; needs an override path that is itself logged.
Effort
1-2 sessions

Option C — Do nothing beyond the existing flags

Buys
Nothing.
Costs
None.
Could go wrong
The staleness found this session recurs and is only caught by a reader noticing.
Effort
None
Why option A: A is cheap and can be done in this session; B is the better control once the refresh path is stable. C leaves a known defect in place.

What you have to do: Confirm A now and whether to fund B later.

D07 · How should the two independent analyses of correlation be reconciled?

ready Verification waiting on: nothing

Different sessions computed the same correlations on different scopes (32 standings rows vs 136 games vs 7,103 games) and published values that cannot be compared. A reader comparing two pages sees two numbers for one concept.

Option A — Publish one correlation register with the scope in the column header recommended

Buys
No two numbers with the same name.
Costs
Low: merge on presentation, keep every underlying computation.
Could go wrong
Requires deleting or relabelling the smaller-scope figures from display.
Effort
1 session

Option B — Keep both and add a comparability warning

Buys
Nothing is removed.
Costs
Low.
Could go wrong
The reader still has to do the reconciliation.
Effort
1 session
Why option A: The correlation question is the one most likely to be quoted out of context. One register with the scope in the header removes the ambiguity at the source.

What you have to do: Confirm A; it can be applied at the next integration.
