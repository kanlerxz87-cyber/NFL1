Injuries & replacement

Three different things live on this page and they are deliberately kept apart: who the official report says cannot play, what that is worth when it is put into a model, and who actually took the snaps.

1. The official designation is a level. It is published, and it is not a signal.

Source: the official nfl.com Week 10 report. 347 rows across 28 clubs. The exact file this page was built from, with its capture time and sha256, is listed on Verification; that page is regenerated whenever the capture is refreshed, so the hash is recorded there rather than frozen here.

176 of 347 rows carry no game designation at all. A blank is not "healthy" and it is not "available" — it is a field the club left empty. Treating it as either would be an invented value, so the study does not use the designation as a level at all. The registered surprise rule (C84-S11) is on the strategy page and it earned 0.05% on 1,002 decided bets, which is indistinguishable from nothing.

2. What the availability features are worth — the ablation

The brief rejects a fixed points penalty and asks for an ablation. This is one: the identical model, refitted with and without two availability columns, scored out of sample.

The availability features add nothing measurable: the interval on the difference contains zero. Interval on the difference: [-0.02263, 0.02163]. A negative number in a window means the availability features made the forecast slightly worse there. A fixed points-per-starter penalty asserts a magnitude. This ablation measures one, and reports the interval, so a reviewer can see whether the measurement is distinguishable from noise.

3. Confirmed replacements, not inferred depth order

The starting quarterback on each game row is the quarterback who took the first snap, carried in the game feed by GSIS player id. It is a record of playing time, not a depth-chart opinion, so a replacement identified here is a CONFIRMED replacement.

Not used: club depth charts (a weekly opinion, and a one-time transcription in this repo); jersey numbers, roster order, salary, draft position or experience; any snap count (no official snap-count source is committed here).
