Executive summary

What was built, the five findings that matter, what is unresolved, what is being asked for.

We built our own lines over 7,239 games and scored 14,440 bets at real, observed prices. Our line is less accurate than the market's, but it still adds information the market does not have. No strategy survived all eight validation gates. That is the finding, and it is the useful one.

What was built

  • A provenance-pinned research extract: 7,239 regular-season games 1999-2026, 24,932 skill-player weeks and 56,537 official injury rows, every source file pinned by URL and sha256.
  • Walk-forward own lines: ridge team ratings refitted for every (season, week) block using only games that finished earlier, with the recency half-life chosen on a validation slice of the training data.
  • A scored leaderboard: twelve rules, 14,440 bets, every bet priced from the source file, eight validation gates, Benjamini-Hochberg correction and an exact luck reference distribution.
  • A correlation register: eleven game-level and eight same-game player-prop correlations, every one with a Fisher-z 95% interval.
  • A parlay matrix: all 66 two-leg combinations priced at the product of their legs, with the realised dependence measured against independence.
  • Verification against official pages: 32/32 standings rows, 136/136 game results, 3/3 injury designations.

The five findings that matter

  • The market line is more accurate than ours. Spread MAE 10.70 points against 10.24 over 6,975 games; the market line is closer to the result in a paired test with t = 10.33.
  • Our line still adds information. Regressing the result on both lines gives our line a weight of 0.161 with t = 3.35. The two facts are not in conflict: a line can be worse overall and still know something the better line does not.
  • Nothing passes. Zero of twelve strategies clear all eight gates. The closest, fading road favourites of seven or more, made 24.3 units where a lucky run would land inside plus or minus 19.0, and would need about 1,950 bets before that edge could be detected.
  • The strongest single result is a loss. Backing the under when the closing total is 40 or lower returned -7.93% over 887 bets (p = 0.015, BH-adjusted 0.178). It is either a real pricing bias or a sample artefact, and the honest response is to pre-register it and wait a season.
  • Parlay legs in different games really are independent. Across 66 combinations the realised joint hit rate divided by the product of the legs' own rates averages 1.0002. Inside one game they are emphatically not: a quarterback's passing yards and his top receiver's receiving yards correlate +0.672.

What is unresolved

  • Player props have no price in any reachable official source, so no prop bet is scored and no prop profit is claimed. The track publishes projections, calibration and correlations only.
  • Historical depth charts are absent, so 'is the backup any good' cannot be back-tested. The current season has them; history does not.
  • The mirrored injury feed lags the official page on game designations by an unknown amount, which caps what any historical injury rule can use.
  • 1,902 played games carry a line but no price. They train the ratings; they are never scored.

What is being asked for

  • Decide whether to blend our line with the market line (suggestion S01) or publish ours raw.
  • Decide whether to fund historical depth charts (S02) or accept that backup quality stays unmeasured.
  • Decide whether the low-total finding is worth pre-registering for a season (S05).
  • Decide whether to consolidate the five site generations, which needs a cross-session request rather than a unilateral edit (S04).
