S01Blend our line with the market line and score the blendOur line is less accurate than the closing line (MAE 10.70 vs 10.24) but adds statistically significant information to it (weight 0.161, t = 3.35). Neither fact alone tells you what to do.
  1. A. Ship the raw model line as the house line — effort: none; risk: High: it is measurably worse than the market and will lose the vig.
  2. B. Publish a market-first blend, weight chosen out of sample — effort: low; risk: Medium: the blend weight must be selected on earlier seasons only, or it is fitted to the answer.
  3. C. Publish both lines side by side and let the reader choose — effort: none; risk: Low, but it hands the decision back to the user with no guidance.
B, with the weight selected by nested walk-forward selection exactly as the half-life already is.data/s9cb/lines.json, incremental_testproposed
S02Add historical depth charts so backup quality can be back-testedThe most-requested feature is knowing whether the second or third string replacement is any good. The current season's depth charts are joined to the injury report, but history is missing, so no backup-quality rule can be validated.
  1. A. Add the historical depth_charts releases to the collector — effort: medium; risk: Low; the join code already exists and the files are large but pinnable by sha256.
  2. B. Proxy backup quality with years of experience and prior snap share — effort: medium; risk: Medium; a proxy is not the thing being measured and must be labelled as a proxy.
  3. C. Leave it and report only listed starters out — effort: none; risk: Low accuracy, no false precision.
A. It is the only option that measures the thing the brief asks for.data/model/availability.json; question Q18proposed
S03Log the official injury page daily and record when each club posts its game designationThe mirrored feed lags the official page on game designations: 6 of 276 week-10 rows carry one. A rule built on game status would silently lose most of its input.
  1. A. Scrape the official page several times a day and store snapshots — effort: medium; risk: Low; the scraper already exists in pipeline/scrape_injuries.py.
  2. B. Use practice status only and drop game status — effort: low; risk: Medium; it throws away the single strongest availability signal.
  3. C. Accept the lag and label every injury rule as practice-only — effort: none; risk: Honest but strictly weaker.
A, with the snapshot time stored on every row so a reader knows exactly how stale a designation is.data/s9cb/injuries.csv vs https://www.nfl.com/injuries/; question Q16proposed
S04Collapse four site generations into one, as a decision rather than by one session acting aloneThere are four preserved site generations (root, docs/, verified-lab/, referee/) plus this one. A reader cannot tell which numbers are current.
  1. A. Keep all generations and add a prominent 'which numbers are current' banner — effort: low; risk: Low, but the confusion stays.
  2. B. Nominate one generation as current and demote the rest to an archive index — effort: medium; risk: Medium; it edits other sessions' pages, so it needs to be logged as a cross-session request.
  3. C. Delete the older generations — effort: low; risk: High; it destroys merged work that the coordination rules exist to protect.
A now, B logged as a cross-session request for the next integration session.Question Q23; COORDINATION.md session tableproposed
S05Re-test the low-total under rule out of sample before anyone acts on itBacking the under when the closing total is 40 or lower lost 7.93% over 887 bets (p = 0.015, BH-adjusted 0.178). That is the strongest single result in the study and it is a loss. It is exactly the kind of result that is a real bias and exactly the kind that is a sample artefact.
  1. A. Pre-register the rule and score it on the next season before using it — effort: low; risk: Low; this is the correct scientific response.
  2. B. Trade the reverse of it now — effort: low; risk: High; it does not survive multiple testing across twelve rules.
  3. C. Ignore it — effort: none; risk: Medium; it may be throwing away the only real finding.
A. Pre-registration is cheap and it is the difference between a finding and a story.data/s9cb/leaderboard.json, S09proposed
S06Buy or build a price feed for player props, or stop listing props as a trackThe leaderboard offers a player-prop track, but no prop price exists in any source this repository can reach, so no prop entry can ever be scored.
  1. A. Obtain a licensed prop price feed — effort: high; risk: Medium; cost and licensing.
  2. B. Keep the prop track for projections and calibration only, with prices left blank — effort: none; risk: Low; this is what the site does today.
  3. C. Remove the prop track entirely — effort: low; risk: Loses the projection and correlation work, which is real.
B now, A only if the projections prove skilful enough to be worth pricing.data/s9cb/props.json, price_status; question Q19proposed
S07Improve the player projections before pricing themAgainst a league-mean baseline the walk-forward projections remove 13.6% of the error for running-back rushing yards, 4.8% for receptions, 0.2% for quarterback passing yards and 0.06% for receiver receiving yards. Two of the four markets are barely better than guessing the league average.
  1. A. Add team pass rate, game total and the opponent's rate as features — effort: medium; risk: Low; all three are knowable before kick-off.
  2. B. Fit a proper distribution, not a point projection, so over/under prices can be implied — effort: medium; risk: Low; needed anyway if prices ever arrive.
  3. C. Publish the current projections with their honest skill numbers — effort: none; risk: Low; this is what the site does today.
C immediately, then A, then B. Do not price a projection that beats the league mean by 0.2%.data/s9cb/props.json, marketsproposed
S08Report the same-game parlay correlations on the bet slip itselfA quarterback's passing yards and his top receiver's receiving yards correlate +0.672. A parlay priced at the product of its legs ignores that entirely.
  1. A. Show the measured correlation next to every same-game combination — effort: low; risk: Low; the numbers are already computed.
  2. B. Refuse to price same-game combinations — effort: low; risk: Low but unhelpful.
  3. C. Price them at the product and say nothing — effort: none; risk: High; it is the standard industry failure mode.
A. It costs nothing and it is the difference between an informed combination and a guess.data/s9cb/props.json, same_game_correlationsproposed

  2. B. Hide any strategy below 200 bets — effort: low; risk: Medium; it hides information rather than explaining it.
  3. C. Leave the leaderboard as a ranked table — effort: none; risk: High; a ranked table of noise invites exactly the wrong conclusion.

  2. B. Add a build-fails-loudly step that opens an issue when the refresh fails — effort: low; risk: Low and worth doing.
