The eight gates

  1. G1: walk-forward / pre-game information only
  2. G2: observed prices on every scored bet
  3. G3: at least 200 scored bets
  4. G4: hit rate beats the break-even implied by the actual prices taken
  5. G5: survives multiple testing (BH-adjusted p < 0.05, 12 rules tested)
  6. G6: positive in a untouched holdout (2024 + 2025)
  7. G7: positive in at least 60% of individual seasons
  8. G8: 95% bootstrap confidence interval on ROI excludes zero

Every rule is declared before it is scored, the number of rules tested is published, and the p-values are corrected for that number. A rule that cannot clear all eight is published as a failure with its effect size, because a failed rule is the useful output.

Removing luck

What is tested against

  • A null in which the observed prices are fair, so expected profit is zero and the variance of a 1-unit bet at decimal price d is d−1. The luck band is arithmetic, not a simulation.
  • A binomial test against the break-even implied by the actual prices taken, not against 50%.
  • Benjamini-Hochberg across all 12 rules, so testing twelve and reporting the best one cannot manufacture a winner.
  • A holdout (2024–2025) the thresholds were never tuned on, and a per-season stability count.
  • A bootstrap interval on ROI that must exclude zero.

What that produces today

  • Best rule: S07, 401 bets, 55.58% hit rate against a 52.30% break-even, +6.06% ROI.
  • Its p-value is 0.198. Twelve rules were tested. It does not survive correction.
  • Its own 2024–2025 holdout is positive, which is why it is the one worth re-testing — not the one to bet.
  • Conclusions: a positive full-sample ROI is not evidence of skill at these sample sizes.
