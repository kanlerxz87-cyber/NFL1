What holds up

Each item is checkable from a named file or an official page.

  • The closing line is a better forecaster than the model on the side market: Brier skill -0.0413, interval entirely below zero on 6,785 games.
  • No strategy clears the eight gates. 0 of 12 clear all eight; the best (S07 Fade the road favourite of 7+) clears 6 of 8 and fails multiple testing and the ROI interval.
  • A coin-flip control loses -2.51% on the frozen corpus. Every published strategy result sits inside its own luck band, so none can be called skilful.
  • The official Week 11 injury report is not published yet: the league address serves Week 10. The committed capture matches the live Week 10 page on every cell checked (220 of 220).
  • 13 of 13 Week 11 venues were read from the official game centre and agree with two independent transcriptions made earlier. One official-to-official naming difference exists for Arrowhead Stadium and is flagged.
  • The depth ladder reconciles: 41 = 38 slots where a backup is pressed into duty plus 3 slots with no available player. No club is projected to start a third-string player this week.

What is missing, and what it blocks

In the brief's order of impact on the next decision.

  • An official price. The NFL publishes no odds, so every ROI is measured against a third-party line (flag BF-11). This gates every backtest number on every page.
  • The actual starter. No Game Book, inactive list or snap-count capture exists for any 2026 game, so a replacement can be projected but never confirmed (BF-10).
  • A price-snapshot store with opening and closing numbers. Without it there is no closing-line value and no way to see which side of a line move a bet is on (Q11).
  • A within-week injury series. Two capture days and zero recorded designation changes means the "surprise" the brief asks for has never been observed (BF-02).
  • One authoritative pre-registration registry. Four exist, with four checkers (BF-06).
  • A recorded terms review before row-level injury data is published (Q21).
