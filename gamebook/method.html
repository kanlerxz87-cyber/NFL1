Method & limits

How each number on this sub-site is produced, what it can support, and what it must never be used for.

Pipeline

  1. pipeline/collect_gamebooks.py inactives --season S --week W fetches the league's dated weekly inactives article, records the UTC fetch time and the sha256 of the raw bytes, and parses club headings into inactive names. Written to data/gamebook/captures/inactives/, append-only.
  2. ... reports --season S --week W fetches the official weekly injury report (https://www.nfl.com/injuries/league/{season}/reg{week:02d}) and keeps the practice participation and the game-day designation, with the column decided by the header name so a reordered table cannot shift a value into the wrong field. Written to .../captures/report/.
  3. ... gamebooks --season S walks the official schedule pages for the games, finds each game's Game Book PDF link on the game centre page, downloads the PDF, hashes it, extracts the summary pages and parses Lineups / Substitutions / Did Not Play / Not Active. The raw text region behind every list is hashed and stored, so a derived row can be checked against the document it came from. Written to .../captures/gamebook/.
  4. tools/build_gamebook_register.py (this site) reads only what is committed and derives the join, the calibration table and the results feed. It never calls the network.
  5. tools/check_gamebook_register.py is the gate: required fields, per-club count pins, one official URL per row, duplicate detection, two-source agreement, and the rule that a value the source does not state is blank and flagged.

Point-in-time rule

A capture may be used as a pre-kickoff snapshot only when its recorded fetch time is earlier than the game's official kickoff. Every capture stores its fetch time, so this is checkable, not asserted. The Week 10 report in this repository was captured on 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16 - after some of those games had been played - so those rows are admissible for retrospective calibration (what a designation meant) and not for replaying what an analyst could have known. The register states this on the capture itself (point_in_time) rather than in prose only.

One capture per week, and why the others are not added to it

The report page is captured under the week it states, not the week the url asked for, and exactly one capture per (season, week) is joined: the one that states the most clubs. 27 capture(s) on disk were ignored for that reason. Measured on 2026-11-16: requests for 2025 weeks 1-18 and 2026 weeks 1-11 all answered HTTP 200 with the season's most recent report, so the league does not archive a past week's report at its week url - a designation history can only be built forward from dated captures, or backward from the dated news pages, which do stay online.

Merging two captures of the same page would be wrong even when both look plausible. One of them held all 347 rows under only 14 of the 28 club headings - two clubs' blocks folded into one - so a player who belongs to Washington also appeared under New York, and joining that union moved the published Questionable cell from n=82 to n=126 for the same week and the same page. The rejected capture is therefore listed, not merged: per-week captures on disk: 2025_reg10: 1 capture(s), 2026_reg10: 2 capture(s).

Deliberate limits

  • No snap counts, no depth rank inferred from a Game Book. The brief forbids it, and the document does not contain snap counts anyway. This register therefore publishes who started, who substituted in, who was active but did not play and who was not active - four states, no share of snaps.
  • No venue, roof or weather register here. That is another session's scope; venue text is stored only as a field of the game row so a disagreement can be flagged.
  • No model change. The availability feature the model consumes lives in another session's files; this register is the evidence a change would be judged on, not the change.
  • Transcription method. The one week transcribed in the sandbox was read from the rendered page text (the fetch tool used by the agent), not from raw HTML bytes; the evidence file sits beside it so a reviewer can diff the rows against the lines they came from. Raw bytes + sha256 come from the Actions runs. Recorded as a flag, the same way an earlier session recorded its capture method.

What clears the remaining gate

Measure B (play rate from the Game Book) needs one Game Book per game in the sample. The collector is written and pinned; it runs hourly on Actions and appends captures. When every game of a captured week has a Game Book with parse_status: ok, the play-rate column fills in and the readiness rows IN-04, IN-15, IN-17 and IN-18 move from Blocked with a count instead of a promise.
