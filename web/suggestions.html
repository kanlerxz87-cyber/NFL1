S32Injuries and depthRun the reserve list as the tie-breaker for 'available', not as another injury count
On the week-10 capture, 83 players sit on an open reserve designation and appear on no injury report row, against 347 report rows. Counting only the report therefore treats 83 names as available. The reserve list should enter availability as a separate, dated state (on a list / not on a list) so the injury surprise stays a surprise about the report and the unavailable set stays complete; depth and snap features then ablate against both. S33Injuries and depthRead the Game Book's four lists as the outcome record, and keep the weekly report as the expectation record
The two documents answer different questions and the site currently conflates them. The report is what a club had to declare by 4 PM Friday; the Game Book is what the club's own scorekeeper printed after kickoff: who was in the line-up, who substituted in, who was active and did not play, who was not active. Joining them turns 'Player X is Questionable' into 'Player X was Questionable, was declared inactive, and his backup took the first snap' - which is the only form of that sentence that can price a line. Cost of the join is one PDF per game, already linked from pages the repository already walks.		Review before implementing: this session wrote the capture and the register (gamebook/, data/gamebook/) and stopped at the model, because the availability key belongs to another session's files.Awaiting review
S34Injuries and depthStop scoring designations from one week and start scoring them from a joined multi-week sample
One round gives n=82 Questionable, n=64 Out, n=10 Doubtful. The declared-inactive rate for Questionable is 18.3% with a 95% interval of [11.4%, 28.0%] - an interval wide enough that a model reading the point estimate will move its lines on noise. The same join is available for every past week because both official documents are published per week and stay online, so the sweep can lift n by roughly an order of magnitude per season at no extra risk; that is the difference between a calibration and an anecdote.		Implement in the scheduled job: .github/workflows/gamebook-sweep.yml already loops weeks for 2025 and 2026; extend the range, let the interval tighten, and do not quote a rate until n is stated next to it on the page (it is, in this register).Awaiting review
S35Governance and verificationGive every scheduled capture an assertion about what page it actually landed on
The league returns the latest published week for a future-week URL with HTTP 200 (F68), which means a weekly job can be perfectly green and still be storing the same page under a new name. An assertion on the page's own label costs one line and turns a silent data-integrity failure into a build failure - which is exactly the drift-alarm rule the brief asks for, applied to the week key instead of the row count.		Implement in this session's collector (done: pipeline/collect_gamebooks.py week_label_mismatch), then hand the same assertion to the owners of pipeline/scrape_injuries.py and pipeline/capture_snapshots.py as the change request logged in COORDINATION.md.Awaiting review

S37Signals and pricingUse the Not Active list as the second string's evidence, since the depth chart cannot be trusted to say who is behind whom
The register records 149 club depth slots that print more than one name at rank 1, and two clubs whose depth pages cannot be parsed at all, so 'who is the backup' is often unanswerable from a depth chart. But the game-day inactive list is an answer to a nearby question that is fully sourced: the player declared inactive behind an Out starter at the same position is the man the club expects to play. That inference needs no rank and no jersey number, and it is available for every game. S38Injuries and depthBuild the designation history forward from dated captures, and use the dated news archive for the past
The plan to lift n by sweeping the past two seasons' report pages is dead: the league serves only its most recent report at the week url (F73), so a back-fill stores 29 copies of one page. The weekly inactives article is different - it is a dated news page and stays at its own url - and it carries the club's own declaration, which is the half of the join that a rate needs. So the achievable design is: designation history from the dated news archive and from snapshots taken from now on, play rate from Game Books (which are archived per game), and no claim at all about past weeks' practice participation.		Review before re-scoping the sweep: the workflow's week loop should become a cadence loop (Wed, Thu, Fri, game day) for the current week, with the archive walk pointed at the news index instead of the injuries url.Awaiting review
