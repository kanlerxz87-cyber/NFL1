The short version

The site is honest, and that is the point. It builds its own NFL lines from play-by-play, tests them against the closing line on games they never saw, tracks official injury status against club depth charts, and publishes every number with a source — including the numbers that say the model does not beat the market.

Three findings carry most of the weight:

  1. The own-line model is not yet good enough to act on. Out of sample it is 9.95 points of error against the closing line's 9.45, and a fitted blend puts a negative weight on the model (-0.328). Publishing its disagreement with the price as an "edge" would be unsupported.
  2. No strategy has earned a rank or a reward. 0 of 12 strategies clear all 8 gates; the best paper score is a negative lower-bound return. The leaderboard is built and empty on purpose.
  3. The starter/backup question is only partly answerable. The league publishes injury status but no depth chart; only club sites publish depth charts. This week 6 of 279 official rows carry a game designation, the rest are practice reports. A "third-string corner is starting" claim can be verified for 2 of 26 clubs.

What is still unresolved

  • No odds feed. Until a licensed price source is connected, every "edge" is a difference from a consensus closing number, not a price that could have been taken.
  • No league depth chart. The league URL returns 404. Thirty clubs still need a transcription before a league-wide backup-exposure index means anything.
  • Thirty-two practice cells vs one designation. The injury feed is mid-week; game statuses arrive later. Treat this week's availability numbers as a practice report, not a lineup.
  • Rewards need counsel. The leaderboard is paper points only.
  • Four site generations coexist. This dashboard is the entry point; the others are linked and preserved.
