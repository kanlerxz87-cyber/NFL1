A correlation_ratio above 1.00 means the two legs landed together more often than independence predicts. Because a standard parlay is priced at the product of the legs, positively correlated legs make the parlay cheaper than it should be -- which is why some pairs show a positive return even though neither leg does on its own. That is a property of the pricing rule, not proof of skill, and it is why every pair is published rather than the best one.

The dependence ratio is the joint hit rate divided by the product of the two legs' own hit rates, measured on exactly the legs that make up these parlays. A value of 1.000 means the product rule priced them correctly.

What this says

  • Across 66 combinations the ratio averages 1.0002. Legs in different games behave as if they were independent, so the product rule is a fair price for a cross-game parlay.
  • Only 1 of 66 combinations survives multiple testing, and the one that does is a losing combination, not a winning one. The profitable-looking pairs are what noise looks like when you print 66 of them.
  • The interesting exception is inside a single game, which this page cannot price. See the correlation and props pages: a quarterback's passing yards and his top receiver's receiving yards correlate +0.672.
