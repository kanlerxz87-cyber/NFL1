What was built

Five things, each traceable to an official page:

  1. A verified results base. 136 completed 2026 games, recomputed into standings and compared cell by cell with the official standings page: 32 clubs × W, L, T, PF, PA — 160 of 160 match. official standings
  2. Own lines. A simple rating system fitted by least squares, with a home-field term, re-fitted every week using only earlier weeks. Week 10 spreads, totals and win probabilities are published with 95% bands.
  3. A rule laboratory. 9 rules, fixed before any outcome was computed, scored under ten gates with bootstrap intervals, Benjamini-Hochberg correction and a permutation test against random bet selection.
  4. Availability tracking. The official injury report (279 rows) joined to official club depth charts (31 of 32 clubs, 2065 player slots), so a missing starter is matched with the second, third or fourth string behind them.
  5. A decision register. 20 questions split into what is known, what is missing, what it blocks and how to discover it, plus 16 suggestions that sit at PROPOSED with options and a recommendation. Nothing has been implemented on purpose — the choice is yours.

What the evidence says

  • The lines are better than nothing, barely. Mean absolute spread error 9.564 points against 10.325 for "always predict the home-field number" and 10.295 for "always predict a pick'em". Real, measured on six weeks of one season, and small.
  • No rule survives the gates. All seven graded rules fail at least one. The closest is the rest edge: 26 bets, 73% wins against my line, permutation p = 0.0005 — and it still fails the 30-bet minimum and the multi-season check. That is the gates working, not the rules being useless.
  • Two gates cannot be evaluated at all. Beating the book's break-even needs prices, and there are none. Multi-season stability needs a second season, and only 2026 has been transcribed and verified.
  • Availability is trackable now, but thin. Only 6 of 279 injury rows carry a game status, so the model leans on practice participation, weighted by depth rank. 76 rows name a player who is not on that club's own depth chart — two official sources disagree, and the lab scores only the 203 they agree on.
  • The biggest published gap from earlier sessions is closed. nfl.com publishes no depth chart (404, re-verified), but the 32 club sites do, and 31 of 32 are machine-readable.
