Three defects you should know about before trusting this feed.
1. It is blind to injured reserve — Seattle shows 'No Injuries Reported' while NFL.com news on the same page reports their starting quarterback ruled out (F-004).
2. The body-part column is empty for 248 of 254 rows (F-003).
3. Game statuses are only filed near the end of the week, so a Thursday snapshot is mostly blank (F-005).

Deficiencies and backups — the hard part

The brief asks the system to know when a 2nd, 3rd or 4th string player is replacing a starter, on both sides of the ball. The league publishes no depth chart. A direct fetch of nfl.com/teams/<team>/depth-chart returns the NFL's 404 page. There is therefore no official field to read, and any depth ordering is an estimate rather than a fact. Flagged as F-001 and addressed by suggestions S-007 and S-008.

Proposed deficiency index

For each club and each position group, compute the share of the group's recent snap share that is unavailable, weighted by the inferred depth of the replacement. Losing 60% of a position group's snaps to a third-string replacement must score worse than losing 20% to a second-string replacement — otherwise the index does not capture what the brief is actually asking for.

What it needs

  • An official or licensed snap-count / participation source. Not yet confirmed.
  • A trailing window for the snap-share ranking. A short window reacts fast but is noisy; a long window is stable but lags a demotion.
  • The official transactions feed, so that injured-reserve placements are counted as unavailable rather than invisible.

Until those exist, the honest output is the practice-participation table above, badged as an incomplete proxy rather than presented as the finished feature.
