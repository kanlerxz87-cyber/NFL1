Why this page is mostly empty

The brief asks us to determine correlation values. We have not invented them. No league-official, government-official or regulator-official source publishes a player-prop correlation matrix, and no historical joint-outcome data has been ingested. Every Magnitude cell is therefore blank and badged UNVERIFIED. That empty column is the honest state of the project, and filling it in with plausible numbers would be the single worst thing this page could do.

What the register does give you is the structural direction of each relationship, which follows from how football works rather than from data, plus the exact estimation method for each pair and the order in which they should be estimated. One value is known without measurement: COR-010, where two moneylines in the same game are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, so the correlation is −1 by definition.

The most important line on this page is the guardrail: a pair whose correlation is unknown is treated as unknown, not as zero. Assuming zero is itself an assumption, and it is precisely the one that misprices parlays.

The arithmetic of combining legs

IDNameExpressionKindNote
ALG-1Parlay price under independenceP(all legs win) = P(leg 1) x P(leg 2) x ... x P(leg n)Arithmetic identityThis is the naive model. It is correct only if the legs are independent, and football props almost never are.
ALG-2Joint probability with a known phi coefficientP(A and B) = P(A)P(B) + rho * sqrt( P(A)(1-P(A)) * P(B)(1-P(B)) )Exact identity for binary variablesThis is the definition of the phi coefficient rearranged. It is algebra, not an estimate - but it needs rho, and rho is exactly what we do not have.
ALG-3Error from assuming independencepricing error = rho * sqrt( P(A)(1-P(A)) * P(B)(1-P(B)) )DerivedThis is the size of the mistake made by naively multiplying. With a moderate positive rho and two legs near even money, the error is material relative to a typical margin. The direction of the harm depends on the sign of rho: positive correlation makes a parlay more likely to win than naive multiplication says, negative correlation makes it less likely.
ALG-4Worked illustration with deliberately synthetic inputsTake P(A) = P(B) = 0.50 and a hypothetical rho = 0.30. Naive independence gives P = 0.25. Applying ALG-2 gives P = 0.25 + 0.30 * sqrt(0.25 * 0.25) = 0.25 + 0.075 = 0.325.ILLUSTRATION ONLYThe 0.50 and 0.30 values are invented solely to demonstrate the arithmetic. They are NOT measurements, NOT estimates from data, and must never be used in pricing. They are shown here because the formula is easier to audit with numbers in it.

Estimation plan

  1. 1. Define the pair universe and freeze it.
  2. 2. Collect historical joint outcomes for every pair over a fixed window.
  3. 3. Estimate the phi coefficient on the binary over/under outcomes, and the Pearson or Spearman coefficient on the continuous underlying statistics.
  4. 4. Hold out a later window and check that the estimated correlation predicts joint hit rates out of sample.
  5. 5. Only then allow the pair into parlay or prop-combo pricing.
  6. 6. Re-estimate every season; publish the version and the window with each value.

Guardrails

  • No pair enters parlay or prop-combo pricing until its magnitude has been estimated and checked out of sample.
  • A pair whose correlation is unknown is treated as unknown, not as zero. Assuming zero is itself an assumption and it is the one that misprices parlays.
  • Every published magnitude carries its estimation window, sample size and version. A number without those is not a result.
  • Where operators refuse a combination outright, record that as a market-structure fact rather than trying to price around it.
