S-001

Tier every data source and refuse to mix tiers

ProposedData quality Effort Low Risk Low
Problem
Values from a league-official page, a government regulator and an open-source community mirror all look identical once they land in a table. A community mirror can silently diverge from the league's own numbers and nothing would flag it.
Proposed change
Label every field with a source tier. Tier 1 = league-official or government-official. Tier 2 = primary issuer or regulated operator. Tier 3 = community. Tier 3 may be referenced but never populates a value.
Evidence needed
A decision on risk appetite, not external data.
Recommended
A now, with B as a later, explicitly gated change.
Source
Owner project brief (this repository)
Options and trade-offs
A. Strict - Tier 1 and 2 only
Only official or issuer-published values may populate a cell.
Pro: Highest trust; the 'no hallucinations' rule becomes structural rather than a matter of discipline.   Con: Slower; some useful data (historical play-by-play) may be unavailable at any price we can justify.
B. Tier 3 allowed behind a re-verification job
Community data may populate a cell only if a scheduled job re-checks it against a Tier 1 source and marks it confirmed or diverged.
Pro: Unlocks large historical datasets.   Con: Adds an engineering dependency and a class of cells whose truth value changes over time.
C. Allow anything, note the source
No tiering.
Pro: Fastest.   Con: Directly contradicts the no-inference rule. Not recommended.
Decision path
  1. Confirm the tier definitions
  2. Decide whether Tier 3 may ever populate a cell
  3. If B, specify the re-verification cadence and the divergence alarm
  4. Implement the tier field in the data layer
  5. Re-run the build-time source assertion

S-003

De-vig market prices before comparing anything

ProposedModelling Effort Low Risk Medium - a wrong de-vig silently biases every downstream edge.
Problem
A book's published prices sum to more than 100% because they contain the margin. Comparing a model probability straight to a raw price manufactures edges that are smaller than the margin, or that do not exist at all.
Proposed change
Normalise the overround to a true probability before any comparison, and record which normalisation was used.
Evidence needed
Which de-vig method best recovers realised frequency is an empirical question. We have not tested it, because we have no odds archive yet (Q-011).
Recommended
A as the default with C run as a sensitivity check; if the two disagree materially, treat the bet as unpriced rather than choosing the friendlier number.
Source
Nevada Gaming Control Board — Gaming Revenue Report
Options and trade-offs
A. Proportional de-vig
Divide each implied probability by the sum of all implied probabilities.
Pro: Simple, deterministic, easy to audit.   Con: Spreads the margin evenly, which is known to be imperfect around favourites and longshots.
B. Equal-margin (margin proportional to odds)
Remove an equal margin weight from each outcome.
Pro: Slightly better behaved at the extremes.   Con: Marginally harder to explain to a reviewer.
C. Shin or odds-ratio method
Model-based removal that accounts for insider trading incentives.
Pro: Best theoretical grounding.   Con: More parameters, more ways to get it wrong silently.
Decision path
  1. Pick a default method
  2. Implement as a pure function
  3. Unit-test on a two-way and a three-way market
  4. Run the sensitivity check
  5. Document which method produced every published number
S-004

Adopt Closing Line Value as the primary skill metric

ProposedValidation & Skill Effort Medium Risk Low
Problem
Profit is dominated by variance. A few hundred bets cannot separate a genuine edge from a lucky run, and waiting for enough bets to know takes seasons.
Proposed change
Score strategies on how far their price beat the closing price, before looking at whether the bet won. Profit remains reported, but it is not the ranking metric.
Evidence needed
A timestamped odds archive including closing prices (Q-011). This is the single highest-value missing input in the whole project.
Recommended
B.
Source
Genius Sports — NFL exclusive official data & Watch & Bet distribution partner
Options and trade-offs
A. CLV is the only ranking metric
Leaderboard ranks purely on average closing line value.
Pro: Available immediately after each bet; not contaminated by result variance.   Con: Measures agreement with the closing market, not truth. If the closing market is wrong, CLV rewards being wrong alongside it.
B. CLV primary, calibration secondary, profit reported
Composite: CLV for ranking, Brier-style calibration as a tiebreak, profit shown but not scored.
Pro: Harder to game; rewards genuine probabilistic accuracy.   Con: More complex to explain.
C. Profit only
Rank on realised return.
Pro: What everyone intuitively wants.   Con: Picks luck, not skill. Directly contradicts the brief.
Decision path
  1. Confirm the definition of the closing price and the timestamp at which it is captured
  2. Implement the CLV capture
  3. Decide the calibration tiebreak
  4. Set the minimum-bets floor
  5. Publish the scoring formula on the methodology page
S-005

Gate every strategy before it is allowed to place anything

ProposedValidation & Skill Effort Medium Risk Low
Problem
Without gates, a strategy that looks good on a small sample gets capital. That is how luck gets mistaken for skill.
Proposed change
Six sequential gates G0 to G5. A strategy must pass all six, in order. Failing any gate returns it to research, it does not get a waiver.
Evidence needed
Enough history to satisfy the sample-size gate. Currently missing (Q-011).
Recommended
A for gates G0-G2 (data integrity, definition, sample size), B for G3-G5 with the waiver recorded publicly.
Source
Nevada Gaming Control Board — Gaming Revenue Report
Options and trade-offs
A. All six gates mandatory, no waivers
Strict sequence.
Pro: Consistent standard.   Con: Slow.
B. Gates with a documented waiver route
A named person may waive a gate in writing.
Pro: Flexibility.   Con: Waivers become routine and the gate stops meaning anything.
Decision path
  1. Ratify the thresholds on the Strategy Lab page
  2. Implement the gate runner
  3. Run every candidate strategy through it
  4. Publish the pass/fail result per strategy
  5. Re-run on every new season
S-006

Build a correlation engine with hard guardrails

ProposedCorrelation Effort High Risk High if skipped
Problem
Parlays and player-prop combos are priced by multiplying legs. That is only correct if the legs are independent. Football props almost never are, so naive multiplication systematically misprices the combination, usually against the bettor for positive correlation and in a way that looks like a gift for negative correlation.
Proposed change
Estimate every pair correlation from data, store it in a versioned register, and refuse to price a combination whose correlation has not been estimated.
Evidence needed
Historical joint outcomes per prop pair (Q-012).
Recommended
A while the register is thin, moving to B once the uncertainty penalty can be calibrated from observed error.
Source
NFL Next Gen Stats
Options and trade-offs
A. Estimate empirically, refuse unpriced combos
No correlation, no price, no bet.
Pro: Prevents exactly the mispricing that makes parlays dangerous.   Con: Blocks a large part of the market until enough history is collected.
B. Estimate empirically, price unpriced combos with a penalty
Allow the bet but haircut the edge by an uncertainty penalty.
Pro: Keeps the market open.   Con: The penalty is arbitrary unless calibrated.
C. Use a published correlation table
Adopt someone else's numbers.
Pro: Immediate.   Con: No official or regulator-published table exists (Q-008). Any such table would be an unverified third-party claim.
Decision path
  1. Define the pair universe
  2. Fix the estimation window and method
  3. Estimate
  4. Hold out and check calibration
  5. Only then allow the combination in the leaderboard
S-007

Infer depth charts from snap share, because no official one exists

ProposedInjury & Lineups Effort High Risk Medium
Problem
The brief asks the system to know when a 2nd, 3rd or 4th string player is replacing a starter. The league publishes no depth chart (F-001), so there is no official field to read.
Proposed change
Derive an ordering from snap-share shares within a position group, refreshed weekly, and label it DERIVED on every surface it appears on.
Evidence needed
An official participation/snap source. Not yet confirmed (Q-012 / REQ-012).
Recommended
A, with C evaluated only if A proves too laggy.
Source
NFL.com — Official Injury Report (Week 10, 2026)
Options and trade-offs
A. Snap-share ranking (trailing games, position-group normalised)
Order players within a position group by recent snap share.
Pro: Uses observable playing time rather than a reporter's opinion; refreshable from box scores.   Con: Trailing window lags a breakout or a demotion.
B. Snap-share ranking plus a coach/beat-report override
Human layer on top.
Pro: Catches mid-week changes faster.   Con: Reintroduces unverifiable third-party claims.
C. Buy a commercial depth chart
Licence one.
Pro: Someone else maintains it.   Con: Not official; provenance unknown; cost.
Decision path
  1. Confirm an official or licensed snap-count source exists
  2. Define the trailing window
  3. Build the ranker
  4. Backtest whether the derived depth predicts snap share next week better than a naive baseline
  5. Only then feed it into the deficiency index
S-008

Publish a lineup deficiency index per position group

ProposedInjury & Lineups Effort Medium Risk Medium
Problem
'This defence is missing two starting cornerbacks' is the actual decision-relevant statement. Neither the injury report nor the standings say it.
Proposed change
For each team and position group, compute how much of the group's recent snap share is unavailable, weighted by the inferred depth of the replacement.
Evidence needed
Same as S-007.
Recommended
Build B now as a visible placeholder clearly badged as incomplete, then A once S-007 lands.
Source
NFL.com — Official Injury Report (Week 10, 2026)
Options and trade-offs
A. Snap-share weighted, replacement depth adjusted
Losing 60% of a position group's snaps to a 3rd-string replacement scores worse than losing 20% to a 2nd-string replacement.
Pro: Directly encodes the brief's 2nd/3rd/4th string requirement.   Con: Depends on S-007 being built first.
B. Simple unavailable-snap-share count
Ignore replacement quality.
Pro: Buildable today from official data alone.   Con: Misses the point; a backup all-pro and a backup practice-squad player score the same.
Decision path
  1. Define position groups
  2. Define the weighting
  3. Validate against a held-out set of games where a starter was known to be out
  4. Publish with the DERIVED badge

S-013

Default to fractional Kelly with a hard per-position cap

ProposedBankroll Effort Low Risk Medium if skipped
Problem
Full Kelly sizing on estimated edges is reckless, because an overestimated edge produces an oversized bet, and the error compounds.
Proposed change
One-quarter Kelly, capped at 1% of bankroll per position, until the owner sets a different policy.
Evidence needed
Bankroll figure from the owner (Q-009).
Recommended
A, with B as a fallback if the edge estimates stay too noisy to trust.
Source
Owner project brief (this repository)
Options and trade-offs
A. Quarter Kelly, 1% cap
As proposed.
Pro: Standard risk control; robust to edge overestimation.   Con: Leaves growth on the table if edges are real.
B. Flat unit staking
Same stake every time.
Pro: Simplest to audit; sizing errors cannot compound.   Con: Ignores edge size entirely.
C. Full Kelly
Maximum theoretical growth.
Pro: Optimal if the edge estimate is exact.   Con: It never is. Not recommended.
Decision path
  1. Confirm the bankroll (Q-009)
  2. Confirm the fraction and cap
  3. Implement as a pure function
  4. Audit on a sample of historical bets
