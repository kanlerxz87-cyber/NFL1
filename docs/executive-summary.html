Executive summary

Purpose. Build a system that originates its own NFL lines, generates and validates betting strategies on a competitive leaderboard, tracks injuries and lineup weaknesses down to backup level, and does all of it on evidence that can be traced to an official source — with an explicit record of what is known, what is unknown, and what decision each unknown is blocking.

What was delivered. A twelve-page static dashboard served from this repository, a JSON data layer for every register, a generator that refuses to publish a data row without a source link, an official-source ingestion of the 2026 Week 10 injury report and the 2026 standings, a 52-item requirement register, a 24-question decision register, 15 suggestions with decision paths, an 18-signal catalogue, six validation gates, a correlation register, and a 12-item flagged-for-review queue.

The honest headline. The analytical framework is complete and specified. The evidence it needs is not yet available. There is no official odds feed, so no strategy has been backtested, no correlation has been measured, and no strategy has passed a gate. That is the current state and it is reported as such rather than papered over.

Recommended next three moves

  1. Obtain a timestamped odds archive. It is the single input that unblocks backtesting, closing-line value, all six validation gates and the leaderboard. Until it arrives, every performance claim is unfalsifiable.
  2. Add the official transactions feed. Without it the injury tracker is blind to injured reserve, which is exactly where the worst injuries go. Proven by flag F-004.
  3. Decide the depth-chart approach. The league publishes none. Either accept a derived snap-share ordering badged as derived, or licence one — but decide explicitly, because the brief's 2nd/3rd/4th-string requirement depends on it.
